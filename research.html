Current Research Projects

Comparative Digital Cultures

This project compares women food influencers’ experiences on Instagram and Xiaohongshu (Red). I examine how they position themselves “in and against the platform”: working within platform rules while also negotiating and resisting them in order to retain agency and creativity. The project also develops the concept of the “emotional contract” to explain influencers’ relationships with brands, particularly the expectations, obligations and guilt that can make under-compensated work difficult to refuse.

Lying Flat & Everyday Resistance

Together with Dr Jiaming Li (University of Birmingham), Dr Edward Granter (University of Birmingham) and Prof Jeremy Aroles (University of York), I examine “lying flat” creators on Chinese social media and ask how everyday resistance can be sustained over time. We track how creators turn anti-work aspirations into narratives and aesthetics, make alternative ways of living materially viable, and continually adapt their practices in response to platform governance and wider social pressures.

Eastern Gaze

Together with Dr Wendi Li (Monash University), I reflect on the challenges of navigating academia as scholars from historically underrepresented backgrounds. Drawing on our everyday experiences of research and teaching, we examine our positionalities within Western institutions, the Western gaze we encounter, and the counter-gaze we turn back to interrogate and challenge coloniality.

Counter Research

As co-founders of CRIMSON, we explore how counter-research should not be understood simply as opposition to forms of power that have historically dominated Western imaginaries. Instead, we approach the “counter” as context-specific, fluid, and negotiated — a space for reworking dominant assumptions about knowledge and research.
